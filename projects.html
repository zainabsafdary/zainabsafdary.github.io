My research lies at the intersection of development and behavioral economics. I am particularly interested in the mechanisms through which macroeconomic conditions, institutional constraints, and individual decision-making jointly determine economic outcomes, and in why those outcomes diverge so sharply across populations.

This page presents brief summaries of the projects I have completed to date, outlining the research question and principal findings of each. It will be updated as new work is completed.

Development Economics

In development economics, I am interested in poverty, labor markets, and migration: what allows economic growth to improve people's lives, and what stands in the way of it reaching everyone.

  • Growth Elasticities, Distributional Shifts, and the Post-2015 Deceleration in Global Extreme Poverty Reduction . Research summary, June 2026.
  • Labor Market Integration of Urban Refugees: Evidence on Employment Outcomes and Barriers to Formal Work in Kampala, Uganda . Research summary, December 2025.
  • Remote Work Mobility and Host-Country Characteristics: An Empirical Analysis of Digital Nomad Destination Choice . Research summary, June 2025.

Behavioral Economics

In behavioral economics, I am interested in how people actually make decisions, and how new technologies change the way we borrow, spend, and learn.

  • Heterogeneity in Buy Now, Pay Later Adoption and Repayment: The Role of Liquidity Constraints and Behavioral Biases . January, February 2026.
  • The Effects of Generative AI Adoption on Skill Acquisition and Human Capital Formation . Research summary, May 2025.
